The Headline Problem (Hook)\n```\nEven \"secure\" services see your data.\n\nGoogle can read your Gmail. Microsoft can read your OneDrive. \nDroboX can read your files. Even password managers like 1Password \nor Bitwarden hold the master encryption key.\n\nBut what if the service couldn't read your data... even if they wanted to?\n```\n\n### 2. What is Zero-Knowledge?\n```\nZero-Knowledge means:\n\nThe service provider has ZERO knowledge of your data.\n\nYour encryption key never leaves your device.\nEven the service itself doesn't have access to decrypt your information.\n\nExample:\n- Password managers: You have a \"master password\"\n- Zero-knowledge vault: Only YOUR device has the encryption key\n\nIf hackers breach our servers, they get:\n❌ No passwords\n❌ No files\n❌ No notes\n✅ Just encrypted blobs (useless without your key)\n```\n\n### 3. Technical Deep Dive\n```\nHow It Works:\n\n1. You enter your password\n2. Your device derives an encryption key (Argon2id)\n3. Everything is encrypted before leaving your computer (XChaCha20-Poly1305)\n4. Encrypted blob + salt → server (can't be decrypted)\n5. When you login, same steps: password → key → decrypt locally\n\nOur servers never see:\n- Your password\n- Your encryption key\n- Your decrypted data\n\nWe CAN see:\n- Encrypted blobs (gibberish)\n- Metadata (file count, storage used)\n- Audit logs (login attempts, user actions - no sensitive content)\n```\n\n### 4. Why It Matters: Real-World Scenarios\n\n#### Scenario 1: Company Breach\n```\nHypothetical: Vault servers are breached.\n\nScenario A (Traditional password manager):\n❌ Hacker gets encrypted database\n❌ Hacker could brute-force master password\n❌ All your passwords exposed\n😱 Accounts compromised\n\nScenario B (Zero-Knowledge):\n❌ Hacker gets encrypted blobs\n✅ No keys on our servers\n✅ Even with our code, can't decrypt\n😊 You're safe (we have zero knowledge)\n```\n\n#### Scenario 2: Subpoena / Legal Request\n```\nGovernment asks: \"Give us user X's passwords\"\n\nScenario A (Traditional):\n❌ Company has to comply\n😱 Your passwords exposed\n\nScenario B (Zero-Knowledge):\n✅ We legally can't comply (we don't have the data)\n✅ We can prove we have zero knowledge\n😊 Your privacy protected\n```\n\n#### Scenario 3: Insider Attack\n```\nA disgruntled employee tries to steal user data.\n\nScenario A (Traditional):\n❌ Employee has internal access to decryption keys\n😱 Could steal everything\n\nScenario B (Zero-Knowledge):\n✅ Even employees can't decrypt your data\n✅ Keys never stored in our systems\n😊 Insider access = still useless\n```\n\n### 5. The Trade-Offs (Honesty)\n\n**Advantages:**\n✅ Maximum privacy\n✅ Hackers get nothing\n✅ Legal immunity\n✅ Client-side encryption\n✅ Perfect forward secrecy\n\n**Disadvantages:**\n⚠️ Forgot password = can't recover (they're really gone)\n⚠️ Slower initial setup (key derivation is intentionally slow)\n⚠️ Less feature-rich (can't do server-side search)\n⚠️ Requires client support (browser/app updates)\n\n### 7. How We Implement It\n\n```\nTech Stack:\n- Encryption: XChaCha20-Poly1305 (AEAD cipher)\n- Hashing: Argon2id (resistant to GPU/ASIC attacks)\n- Key Derivation: PBKDF2 + Argon2id\n- Database: PostgreSQL (stores only encrypted blobs)\n- Auditing: Audit logs (zero sensitive data)\n```\n\n### 8.
